- **Responsibility:** cutoff-safe `LIQUIDITY_SPIKE` detection, candidate-first risk gating and immutable accepted ENTRY signal evidence for the first slice.
- **Owned data:** `signal.signal_candidates` and `signal.accepted_signals`. Candidates have an explicit deduplication identity, status and version/fingerprint evidence; accepted signals retain source normalized identities, decision-time risk facts, exact score/confidence and versioned JSONB reasoning.
- **Public API:** `SignalApi` accepts a dataset fingerprint, asset, decision window/cutoff, validated risk facts and detector/scorer/configuration identities; it returns immutable candidate and accepted-signal snapshots and supports accepted-signal lookup.
- **Allowed dependencies:** `kernel::api`, `marketdata::api`, `risk::api`, `wallet::api`; the implemented path uses only the first three.
- **Transactions:** market-data reads happen before writes. A short signal transaction commits or resolves `DETECTED`; risk assessment runs outside it; a second signal transaction atomically records `REJECTED` or `ACCEPTED` and the optional accepted snapshot.
- **First-slice rule:** latest decision-time liquidity must be at least 1.50 times the deterministic one-hour baseline and at least USD `10000.00000000` higher. `ALLOW` produces score `70`, grade `B`, confidence `1.0000` and factors `20 + 30 + 20`.
- **Fixed observation policy:** new detection accepts only `liquidity-spike-v2`. After UTC microsecond normalization, `C` is the decision cutoff, `T = C - 60 minutes`, and `windowStart` must equal `T`. Select the latest baseline by `observedAt` in inclusive `[T - 5 minutes, T]` and latest current observation in inclusive `[C - 1 minute, C]`, restricted to the requested asset, immutable dataset and cutoff. Equal-time selection retains the market-data API order: timestamp, chain, transaction value, event locator, with textual keys in PostgreSQL bytewise `C` order.
- **Coverage and validation:** require an eligible observation in each window, with distinct normalized identities and observation timestamps. Missing/stale endpoints or unmet growth thresholds return no candidate or accepted signal without signal writes; windows are never widened and liquidity is never interpolated. Unsupported detector versions, mismatched normalized targets and unrepresentable bounds fail before market-data queries, risk assessment or writes. Request shape and caller configuration fingerprint are unchanged; the new detector version distinguishes reproducible identity. Historical v1 snapshots remain readable without rewriting or executing the old detector.
- **Invariants:** public window, decision and risk cutoffs are normalized to microseconds before validation, identity, querying and persistence; no observation after the decision cutoff participates; risk evidence must match the selected asset, cutoff and decision-time liquidity; equal detection is idempotent and immutable conflicts fail; signal never writes or directly reads another module's tables.
- **Interpretation limits:** this compares two endpoint observations 59–65 minutes apart. The five-minute baseline tolerance and one-minute current bound are fixed initial engineering choices, not empirically optimal calibration. `observedAt` bounds establish observation-time recency, not source-event freshness; `sourceEventTime` remains provenance. Two endpoints do not prove sustained growth, continuous hourly coverage or absence of provider gaps. Comparable liquidity evidence remains the caller's responsibility; no cross-venue/pool aggregation is introduced.
- **Non-goals:** other signal families, wallet-derived scoring, provider ownership, shadow outcomes, statistical edge claims, virtual positions and execution. F1 provider selection, F3 availability/gap handling and complete production F6.2 coverage remain outside this bounded recorded-data change.
- **Main tests:** exact window boundaries and microsecond exclusions, stale/missing endpoints, normalized target and detector-version rejection, threshold boundaries, dataset/asset/cutoff isolation, bytewise Unicode ties, risk rejection, candidate-first durability, immutable scoring/lineage, historical v1 lookup, equal retry and PostgreSQL V3 ownership/constraints.
